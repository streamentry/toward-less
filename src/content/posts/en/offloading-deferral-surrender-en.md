Addy Osmani’s 3 November 2026 X article asks how to keep judgment while working with agents. He describes three positions:

- **Offloading:** delegate a task but own the result.
- **Deferral:** let the model’s output become your next input without checking it as a hypothesis.
- **Cognitive surrender (a term Osmani credits to Steven Shaw and Gideon Nave):** accept the output as your own without meaningful review.

The slide can be gradual. Osmani argues that polished answers and passing checks make it easy to stop looking closely. This is his working model, not an empirical result established here.

## Match review to the stakes

- Delegate choices where a mistake is cheap.
- Test decisions that affect users, safety, money, or other people. Ask what the check proves and what it misses.
- You do not need a full mental model of a large codebase. Rebuild the part you need from code, tests, logs, or explanations.
- Write your own hypothesis and definition of done before prompting. If you cannot explain why the result is sound, do not approve it.

Keep enough review time to stand behind what ships. If agent output grows faster than you can verify it, improve the checks or slow down. Responsibility stays with the person who approves the result.
