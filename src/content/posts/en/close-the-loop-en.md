A linked X post argues that finishing tasks and keeping promises can take you far. The popular claim behind it—that unfinished tasks are remembered better—is not reliable.

## What studies show

- A 2025 meta-analysis found no overall memory advantage for interrupted tasks: the weighted recall ratio was 0.99 across 38 publications. But interrupted tasks were resumed about 67% of the time across 21 publications. Remembering and resuming are different. [Ghibellini and Meier](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w).
- In 2011 experiments, Masicampo and Baumeister found that unfinished goals could intrude on unrelated tasks. Making a specific plan for when and how to act reduced those thoughts and the interference. The plan did not reduce anxiety or restore positive feelings; it quieted goal-related thoughts without completing the task. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/).

## Close the loop

For each open task, choose one:
- finish it;
- make a specific plan for what, when, and where;
- cancel it or decline, and tell anyone who is waiting.

The experiment directly tested specific planning, not cancellation. “I’ll do it soon” is not the kind of plan it studied.
