Dry-roasted soybeans are a convenient snack when a meal is not available. Edamame is soybean harvested before maturity; a green dried bean is not enough to identify its harvest stage. [1](https://extension.sdstate.edu/edamame-harvest-and-storage)

One commercial sea-salt roasted edamame label lists 130 kcal, 14 g protein and 6 g fiber per 30 g. [2](https://www.directionsforme.org/product/514059) Protein and fiber make it a reasonable choice for hunger, but roasted beans are energy-dense and these figures do not apply to every brand.

## What about isoflavones?

Soy contains isoflavones, plant compounds that can bind estrogen receptors. They are not human estrogen. A 2021 meta-analysis of 41 studies found no significant changes in men's testosterone or estrogen levels. [3](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33383165/) A smaller 2025 review of randomized trials also found no significant overall testosterone effect, but its dose-response models suggested possible changes in estradiol and sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG). The limited evidence warrants caution with concentrated supplements, not fear of ordinary soy foods. [4](https://doi.org/10.1002/fft2.70090)

A 2019 analysis found that soy protein lowered LDL cholesterol by about 3–4% on average; this does not establish the same benefit from isoflavones alone or one snack. [5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006811/)

Choose dry-roasted, lightly salted or unsalted beans. Portion 25–30 g and drink water. Avoid soy if allergic. Keep the pack cool and dry; a hot parked car is not a pantry. [6](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-long-can-you-keep-canned-goods)
