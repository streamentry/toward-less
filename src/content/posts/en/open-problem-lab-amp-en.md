AI can generate plausible antimicrobial peptide (AMP) candidates quickly. Plausibility is not evidence of biological activity, and a wrong result can waste lab effort or mislead a decision.

[Open Problem Lab](https://github.com/Open-Problem-Lab/open-problem-lab) is building a GitHub-native workflow for the steps between a candidate and an experiment. The current repository is deliberately dry-lab: toy data, transparent baseline scoring, checks for novelty and feasibility, safety-risk flags, evidence records, and reproducibility tools. It does not claim that software can prove a peptide works; wet-lab assays are still required.

Work is split into scoped tasks with done conditions. A result enters the shared record only after structured validation and human review. Quantitative or safety-sensitive claims also need independent reproduction. AI can help triage, compare, rank, and document candidates, but it does not approve its own conclusions.

The longer-term aim is to help qualified scientists choose fewer, more informative experiments and learn from their results. That is a goal, not a result the current dry-lab pipeline has established.
