Stijn Noorman’s article presents five pillars of strategic thinking: vision, analysis, judgment, execution, and adaptation. It is a useful summary of familiar planning tools, not a new or tested theory.

- Vision: break a long-term aim into smaller actions.
- Analysis: assess your resources, strengths, weaknesses, and conditions.
- Judgment: compare trade-offs and choose a path.
- Execution: focus effort on the chosen work.
- Adaptation: use feedback to revise the plan.

The chess analogy has limits. Chess has a fixed board, complete information, and a shared win condition. Real decisions involve unknowns, other people, and goals that may change. The framework describes how to pursue a goal; it gives little help choosing or revising the goal itself.

The article’s worked example is a creator business targeting $10,000 a month. The arithmetic is correct: at $500 per sale, it needs 20 sales; at a 2% conversion rate, 1,000 offer views; if 1% of content viewers reach the offer, about 100,000 views. But the conversion rates are hypothetical inputs, not measured results. Test them before building a plan around them.

The article also draws a leverage chart from 1× to 1,000× without a cited measurement. Treat that as an illustration, not a scale. Its linked X post could not be reloaded for this revision, so direct quotes and sales-pitch details are omitted.

Use the five pillars to organize a plan, then check the assumptions and keep the goal open to change. A strategy is useful only while it fits the world and the life you want.
