The 2024 Lancet Commission estimated a weighted population-attributable fraction of 45.3% for 14 potentially modifiable dementia risks. It said this share could theoretically be prevented or delayed if the risks were eliminated. This is a population model, not one person’s odds.

- **Early life:** less education.
- **Midlife:** hearing loss, high LDL, depression, head injury, inactivity, diabetes, smoking, hypertension, obesity and excess alcohol.
- **Later life:** social isolation, air pollution and untreated vision loss.

The 2024 report added high LDL and untreated vision loss to the 12 factors listed in 2020. Its model adjusts for overlap among risks, but assumes causal links. The Commission says estimates may be too high or low; it also says the figure does not guarantee that any individual will avoid dementia.

Several factors depend on education, healthcare access, living conditions and public policy—not only personal choices. Read 45.3% as a reason to support prevention across a lifetime, not as a promise or personal prescription.
