A social post presents ashwagandha as a way to lower cortisol and “stop caring.” The clinical evidence is narrower, and extracts are not interchangeable.

- In a 2012 trial, 64 adults with chronic stress took placebo or 300 mg of KSM-66 root extract twice daily for 60 days. Cortisol fell 27.9% with the extract and 7.9% with placebo; perceived-stress scores fell 44% and 5.5%. This was a small, single-center trial of one standardized product.
- A 2024 meta-analysis of nine trials found lower average stress, anxiety, and cortisol measures. A 2025 review of seven cortisol studies and six perceived-stress studies (488 participants) found lower cortisol but no statistically significant change in perceived stress. The reviews included different studies, so the effect people feel remains uncertain.
- NIH’s Office of Dietary Supplements says short-term use is generally tolerated, but long-term safety is unknown. It notes mild side effects and reports of liver injury, thyroid effects, and medication interactions. LiverTox advises people with cirrhosis or advanced chronic liver disease to avoid it.

Lower cortisol is not the same as feeling less stressed, and neither means “not caring.” One 60-day trial of one extract does not establish a general dose. The evidence supports further study, not a universal protocol. If you take medication, have liver or thyroid disease, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, consult a clinician before use.
