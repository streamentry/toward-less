I want to begin Marcel Proust’s In Search of Lost Time with its first volume, then decide whether to continue. That is the whole commitment.

Nikita Huno’s 28 October 2026 essay “There is still time, after all” describes reading Proust and being drawn into the narrator’s reflections on memory. He wonders whether those passages would have affected him as much without the story around them.

That gives me a question I can answer only by reading: what does the story contribute to those passages?

- Read a passage myself before asking AI to explain it.
- Note where I pause, what I miss, or what I disagree with.
- Choose a translation I can follow, set aside uninterrupted time, and let the first volume be enough for now.

Reading technical documentation for a specific problem has a clear goal: understand it, find an answer, and use it. With Proust, I do not want to decide in advance how the book must help my work or what lesson it must provide.

Huno also writes that after years of writing for outcomes, he wants to relearn essays that follow a question without knowing the ending. His eight-year reading plan is flexible. That is his experience. I like the long view, but will not start with a huge list; making one is easier than reading.

A summary can explain a book’s themes; it cannot tell me how its sentences and scenes feel while I read them. I might find the book difficult. If so, I can slow down or pause. I do not need to promise that I will finish it.

My next note should begin with pages I have read, not a title I saved.
