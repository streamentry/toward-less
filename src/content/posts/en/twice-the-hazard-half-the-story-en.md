A 2026 observational study reported a higher dementia hazard among statin users in two cohorts. The size of an association does not establish that statins caused it.

## What the study found

- It analyzed 83,355 people in the U.S. All of Us cohort and 173,282 in UK Biobank. Participants were adults aged 40 or older who self-identified as White.
- Adjusted hazard ratios were 2.22 in All of Us and 2.54 in UK Biobank. High-intensity use had a 1.14 hazard ratio versus moderate-to-low intensity.
- The paper also reports weighted incidence rates of 64.9 versus 30.7 per 10,000 person-years. These are cohort rates, not a person’s absolute risk over a defined period.

## What it cannot establish

- The study is retrospective and observational. The authors say confounding by indication may remain: the cardiovascular risks that lead to statin prescriptions can also relate to dementia. Weighting balances measured factors, not everything that matters.
- Dementia was identified from diagnosis codes. The study’s non-user group was also defined using information collected after the index date, which may introduce selection bias.
- The COQ7 analysis is exploratory. It attributes 8.2% of the association to a statistical pathway, but direct CoQ10 was unavailable, COQ7 was measured once during treatment, and there were no pre-treatment measurements. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.

The authors say these results need further study before they inform clinical decisions. Two cohorts show a repeated association, not proof of cause. The paper alone is not a reason to stop or change a prescribed statin; discuss treatment changes with the prescribing clinician.
