Comparing Buddhist texts across languages invites a tempting shortcut: treat parallels as copies of one original. [Buddhist Textual Witnesses](https://github.com/streamentry/buddhist-textual-witnesses) is built to avoid that.

The repository creates a source-aligned catalog of witnesses across Sanskrit, Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit, Prakrit and Gāndhārī, and Classical Chinese. It pins upstream corpora and exact revisions, and records each work, language, witness, and source. A manuscript, modern edition, translation, and reconstruction remain different kinds of evidence. Pāli can serve as an alignment baseline when useful, but is not treated as the root of every tradition.

Its crosswalks also use careful labels. A full textual parallel is not necessarily an exact parallel: independent recensions can share an ancestor and still differ. Machine-generated or model-reviewed matches remain candidates until human review and explicit promotion. Some sources are registered but not mirrored while redistribution rights remain unclear.

The aim is infrastructure for comparison: preserve what each witness says, where it came from, and what remains uncertain. The project does not claim that one reconstructed Buddhist canon exists.
