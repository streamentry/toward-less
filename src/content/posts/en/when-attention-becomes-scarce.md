A circulating essay argues that the most valuable skill in technology may now be showing up: meeting someone for coffee, remembering a conversation, replying personally rather than with a bot. That is a provocative interpretation, not evidence that “ecosystem lead” is the hottest job or that manual accounts grow fastest.

## Abundant messages, scarce attention

AI reduces the cost of drafting emails, posts and pitches. It does not remove the recipient’s limited time. When supply rises faster than attention, one more generic message often becomes easier to ignore.

There is evidence of a trust problem. In a [2026 Gartner survey of 1,006 US consumers](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-22-gartner-marketing-survuey-finds-35-percent-of-consumers-rely-on-influencers-less-due-to-ai), **57% said AI-generated marketing made them less trusting of brand messages**, while **43% said they rely more on real people** for shopping information and recommendations. These are self-reported attitudes in one market, not proof that every personal interaction outperforms automation.

Relationships can also carry real opportunities. A [large randomized LinkedIn study published in *Science*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107999/) found that moderately weak professional ties could help job mobility. That supports the value of networks, but it does not establish that dinners beat other channels for acquiring customers.

## Human does not automatically mean useful

An expensive dinner can produce little more than photographs and a stack of business cards. A personal message can be irrelevant. And good automation can help people more promptly than overloaded teams.

The real distinction is not **human versus machine**. It is **attention that responds to a particular person's situation versus activity that merely imitates attention**.

For a small founder-led business, that suggests a disciplined approach: use AI for background research, notes, scheduling, and routine follow-up; preserve personal judgment for listening, diagnosis, difficult questions, and commitments. After a conversation, record what changed: a problem understood, an introduction offered, a pilot agreed, or a reason not to pursue the deal.

## Give attention without turning people into funnels

There is another danger: treating every relationship as a potential transaction. People notice when curiosity is just prospecting in disguise. Genuine attention may produce no commercial return, and it can still be worthwhile.

In a Buddhist sense, attentive presence is not simply a higher-performing growth tactic. It is a practice of noticing another person without immediately reducing them to our goals. That does not prevent building a business. It changes how we build one.

**Automate the mechanical. Keep the relational human.** Not because human work is inherently superior, but because the right kind of attention requires understanding, responsibility, and a willingness to be changed by what we hear.
