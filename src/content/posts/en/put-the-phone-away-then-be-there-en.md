Putting a phone in another room is a useful parenting habit. It is not a definition of good parenting.

A post by Dan Go argues that a first step toward being a great parent is keeping the phone far away. The appeal is clear: notifications divide attention, even when we intend to listen.

But removing the device is only the beginning. We can be physically beside a child while mentally rehearsing work, correcting every move, or waiting for playtime to end. Presence is not measured by the distance to a screen.

Try one small ritual: for ten minutes, leave the phone elsewhere, let the child choose what to do, and listen without turning the moment into a lesson. If an urgent call is expected, explain that honestly rather than pretending to be fully available.

The goal is not to be an endlessly available parent. Children also benefit from seeing adults work, rest, and keep sensible boundaries. The goal is to make some shared time genuinely shared.

**Less screen. More attention. No need for perfection.**
