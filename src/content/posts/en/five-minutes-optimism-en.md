The future is uncertain. Yet we often rehearse only what could go wrong.

In a 2011 study, Meevissen and colleagues asked people to imagine their *best possible self* for five minutes daily over two weeks. Compared with a group imagining ordinary activities, their optimism scores increased more. That does not prove their lives improved, or that the effect lasts.

Try it today: imagine a realistic, worthwhile future for your health, relationships and work. Picture one ordinary day there. Then ask: **What is one small thing I can do now?**

This last question is my addition, not a tested component of that experiment. Optimism is not a prediction or an obligation to feel happy. Let the image point toward action; return to the present.

---

**Research:** Meevissen, Peters & Alberts (2011), *Become more optimistic by imagining a best possible self: Effects of a two week intervention*. [Journal of Behavior Therapy and Experimental Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbtep.2011.02.012).
