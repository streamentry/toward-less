A linked X post advises replaying past successes when fear hits and cites a 2014 paper. The paper tested something else: using your name or “you,” rather than “I,” while reflecting on a stressful feeling. It did not test success memories. [Kross et al.’s paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/).

## What the study found

Seven studies (N = 585) examined this kind of self-talk under social stress:
- Before a first-impression conversation and a public speech, participants who used non-first-person language received higher performance ratings from independent observers and reported less distress. After the speech, they also reported less repetitive review of the event.
- In two studies of future social stress, they saw the situation as more of a challenge and less of a threat.
- A meta-analysis found that trait social anxiety did not change these results.

## What it does not show

The experiments used brief, controlled social tasks. They did not test replaying past successes, everyday fears, or treatment for anxiety. Participants were asked to examine their feelings and consider why they felt that way. The authors said the method still needs testing in daily life and clinical populations.

A cautious way to try it is to address yourself by name or as “you” before a stressful conversation, while examining what you feel. The study supports this narrow technique in similar tasks; it does not promise an outcome or replace preparation.
