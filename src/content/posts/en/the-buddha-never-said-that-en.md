A viral X post attributes a guest-house line about sadness, anger, and worry to the Buddha. I found no sutta source for those words.

SN 36.14 uses a similar image: travelers lodge in a guest house, as different feelings arise in the body—pleasant, painful, neutral, worldly, and unworldly. The sutta describes kinds of feeling. It does not tell readers to welcome emotions, wait for them to leave, or stop them taking over.

A related image appears in Rumi’s *Mathnawi*, Book V, section 157. Coleman Barks’s English version, “The Guest House,” draws on scholarly English translations; Barks said he did not work from the Persian. This is a source for a similar image, but it does not prove the X post borrowed from it.

The advice may work as a modern reflection, but it is not what SN 36.14 says. A metaphor also cannot replace human support when someone is in crisis.
