Not every unknown is the same:

- Some uncertainty can be reduced by checking facts, asking a question, or making a plan.
- Some remains after reasonable checks. Waiting for certainty can delay a reversible, low-risk decision.

Ask yourself:

- What is the cost if I am wrong?
- What information could change my decision?
- Can I take one small, reversible step and review the result?

Caution is not always avoidance; moving ahead is not always courage. Match your response to the stakes. The useful skill is telling what can be checked from what must be carried, not treating comfort with uncertainty as a direct measure of life quality.
