On 23 November 2026, Novartis announced that the Oxford-led Phase 3 ORION-4 trial met its primary endpoint. Inclisiran (Leqvio), added to standard lipid-lowering care, reduced a composite of major cardiovascular events among people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. This is an important positive *topline* result, not yet a full trial report.

## What was tested

ORION-4 randomized 16,124 participants to inclisiran or placebo. The primary outcome combined coronary heart disease death, heart attack, fatal or nonfatal ischemic stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization. Inclisiran is a small interfering RNA treatment that reduces production of PCSK9 and lowers LDL cholesterol. Injections are given initially, again at three months, and then every six months—not just two injections in the first year.

## The missing numbers matter

Novartis described the primary result as statistically significant and clinically meaningful. It did not disclose the hazard ratio, absolute event rates, absolute risk reduction, number needed to treat, component-specific effects or detailed adverse-event rates. A positive composite endpoint does not establish that each component improved individually. Nor can we calculate the benefit for an individual patient from a press release.

## What happens next

The company plans full results in the first half of 2027. We need independently inspectable numbers, longer-term safety, cost and access information, and comparison with established lipid-lowering options. The trial tested inclisiran *added to* usual care, not a replacement for statins, and not a universal prevention strategy for healthy people.

Promise is not proof of magnitude. Keep current medicines unchanged unless a treating clinician advises otherwise, and judge the treatment after the full outcome data are available.
