A screenshot of an AI conversation proposes an unusual exercise: instead of picturing the triumphant day when a goal is achieved, write a diary entry about an ordinary Tuesday five years later. Describe the inbox, fatigue, chores, and quiet routines that success would bring.

The idea is useful, but its explanation goes too far. There is no established evidence that this exact exercise “tricks the nervous system” or guarantees achievement. The claim that every pleasant fantasy creates a dopamine reward that weakens motivation is also too sweeping.

## Want the Tuesday, not just the trophy

Imagine wanting to run a company. Not the launch announcement: the customer complaints, payroll, hiring decisions, and evenings when the work remains unfinished. Imagine becoming fit: not the photograph, but the repeated training and sore muscles. If the ordinary day looks unbearable, perhaps the goal is borrowed from someone else's idea of success.

This is not an invitation to rehearse suffering. It is a way to price the hidden obligations of a desired life—including what they may cost your family, health, and attention.

## What research actually supports

Psychologists call one related method **mental contrasting**: picture a desirable outcome, then identify the obstacles in current reality. Pair it with an **implementation intention**, a concrete if–then response to an obstacle. A [2021 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202) across 21 studies and 15,907 participants found a small-to-moderate association with improved goal attainment (Hedges' g = 0.336); an adjustment for possible publication bias reduced the estimate to 0.242. This evidence concerns mental contrasting plus if–then planning—not the “future Tuesday” exercise itself.

## A ten-minute experiment

Write four lines:

1. **Tuesday:** What does a normal day look like after success?
2. **Price:** Which recurring inconvenience comes with it?
3. **Obstacle:** What prevents you from living a small part of that day now?
4. **If–then:** “If [specific obstacle] happens, then I will [specific action].”

Then do one observable action today. If the goal is writing, write 200 words. If it is a healthy body, arrange tomorrow's walk. If it is a business, speak with one prospective customer. Review what actually happened after seven days.

A vision can orient us. It cannot substitute for feedback. The question is not merely *Can I picture the future?* It is *Am I willing to live its ordinary Tuesday?*
