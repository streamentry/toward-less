Our parents knew us before we had words for ourselves. Our partners met us somewhere along the way. Our children may never know the person we were before they arrived.

Each knows something true. None knows the whole story.

A post by @Lovandfear offers a comforting thought: you are the only person who will witness every version of yourself. But even that is not quite true. We forget. Memory edits. We become strangers to our former certainties.

In Buddhist practice, this is not a defect to fix. Impermanence is not only what happens to the people and things around us. It happens here, too. There is continuity without an unchanging owner of the story.

So perhaps the invitation is not to preserve every version of ourselves. It is to meet this one with care.

Be gentle with the child you barely remember. Be honest about the person you are today. Leave room for the person you have not yet become.

And look again at those beside you. Your parents are changing. Your partner is changing. Your children are changing faster than you can notice.

**We do not need to know someone completely to love them well. We need to notice them while they are here.**
