Free work is a trade. Before accepting it, decide what it should buy: a client, attention you control, portfolio proof, or a skill. If the return and stopping point are unclear, the deal is unclear.

Tuan Le’s article frames the return in four categories: client, attention, proof, and skill. The framework is useful, but it does not make unpaid work automatically wise. Keep it bounded:

- Offer one small sample if a paid project may follow; agree on the next step first.
- If you have no client, make a portfolio project and own the result.
- Count “exposure” only when you control the proof and can assess its reach.
- Set a time limit. Ask for a paid next step or referral before starting.

Le reports contacting 400 restaurants, getting 10 replies, and closing 5 paid jobs: a 2.5% reply rate and 1.25% conversion rate. The X Article could not be fetched for this revision, so those figures remain self-reported and unverified independently. They describe one account, not the odds for every freelancer.

VnExpress reports that ShortsCut made US$1.08 million in revenue and more than US$488,000 in net profit in 2025. It also reports that Le offered small shops 10 videos for $2,000, with a refund if none went viral. That was a paid offer with a guarantee, not free work. VnExpress’s opening says the company was founded in 2023; later it says Le founded it at the end of 2022 and launched it in January 2023. The exact start date is unclear.

These reported results show a business can grow; they do not prove free work caused the growth or that others can repeat it. A strategy that requires months without pay also requires a financial runway. If you cannot afford that cost, declining is sound judgment, not a failure of ambition.
