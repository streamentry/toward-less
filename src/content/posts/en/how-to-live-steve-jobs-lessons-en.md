Steve Jobs offered memorable advice on how to live. But one extraordinary entrepreneur's life is not a universal blueprint. The useful question is not how to become Jobs; it is what deserves our finite attention.

**Connect the dots.** His calligraphy class later shaped the Macintosh. Curiosity can create unexpected options. Yet we connect failed paths far less often in hindsight. Trusting the future is courage, not a guarantee that everything happens for a reason.

**Follow curiosity.** Learn things that do not promise a return. Exploration broadens what is possible. But protect the resources and responsibilities that let you keep exploring.

**Love your work.** Meaningful work can sustain effort through setbacks. It does not follow that everyone must find one perfect calling, or that passion excuses exhaustion and neglect of loved ones.

**Remember death.** Mortality can expose vanity and inherited expectations. It should also make us less careless with health, family and obligations. A finite life is a reason to choose carefully, not to gamble recklessly.

**Live your own life.** Independent judgment matters. So do promises, care and honest feedback. Rejecting conformity is not the same as rejecting responsibility.

**Say no to focus.** Jobs argued in 1997 that real focus requires refusing good ideas. Every yes spends time and attention. The harder skill is saying no without using focus as an excuse to avoid uncomfortable but necessary duties.

**Stay hungry, stay foolish.** Keep the beginner's willingness to learn and revise. But do not let perpetual hunger turn into perpetual dissatisfaction. Enough is not defeat.

The seven themes are a synthesis, not one Jobs speech. Most draw on his 2005 Stanford commencement address; the focus remarks come from a 1997 Apple developer conference. The best test of this philosophy is modest: does it improve how you use an ordinary Tuesday?
