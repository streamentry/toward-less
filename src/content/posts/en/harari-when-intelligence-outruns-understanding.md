In [*On the Eve of Abundance*, a November 18, 2026 conversation with Mustafa Suleyman](https://humanistreview.ai/video/suleyman-harari/), Yuval Noah Harari describes a future in which people survive but lose the collective ability to understand the systems governing their lives. He fears what he calls **collective dementia**: finance, politics and science could continue, while their controlling mechanisms become unintelligible to humanity. This is a warning about a possible future, not a finding that it has arrived.

## The problem is accountability, not complexity

No one person understands the entire world economy today. We depend on distributed expertise, records, independent verification and institutions able to correct mistakes. Complexity is governable when those mechanisms remain effective.

The danger is a system whose decisions cannot be meaningfully **understood, tested, challenged, stopped or replaced**. A person clicking an approval button on an opaque AI recommendation may preserve the appearance of oversight while losing actual control.

Four practical questions can guide an assessment:

- **Understand:** Can qualified people trace the consequential data, assumptions and decisions?
- **Verify:** Can independent parties reproduce important evaluations and investigate failure?
- **Override:** Can accountable humans stop or reverse consequential actions?
- **Exit:** Can we replace the system without unacceptable disruption?

These are editorial criteria, not a validated universal measurement scale. Transparency alone is insufficient without the ability to act.

## The strongest counterargument

In the same conversation, Suleyman argues that AI might dramatically increase knowledge and material abundance. It can also help humans read technical documents, inspect programs and detect errors. Harari acknowledges that a bad outcome is not inevitable. Distributed human understanding might grow faster with AI assistance rather than collapse.

The open question is therefore whether **human institutions can expand their capacity to verify and govern as quickly as AI systems expand their influence**.

## The quieter personal risk

One can become more productive with AI while growing less practiced at deciding what is worth doing. Fluent explanations invite passive agreement. Mindful attention asks a different question: what can I observe, check and take responsibility for, here and now?

The Buddhist concern is not that intelligence exists outside the mind, but that we cling to persuasive stories and stop looking carefully. AI is valuable when it makes thinking clearer, not when it becomes a substitute for judgment.

**Use AI to extend understanding, not surrender agency.** Build tools and institutions that remain open to questions, correction, intervention and replacement.
