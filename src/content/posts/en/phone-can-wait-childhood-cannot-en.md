A phone does not have to ring to pull a parent away. Even a quick glance can interrupt a story, a question, or a quiet moment together.

Dan Go suggests putting the phone in another room as a first step toward better parenting. The suggestion is deliberately stark. It points to a real problem, but it is not a measure of whether someone is a good parent. Parents also need phones for work, safety and care.

What matters is not perfect availability. It is reliable attention. When we choose to be with a child, can we listen without repeatedly leaving the moment?

Try one small experiment: choose 20 minutes each day, leave the phone out of reach, and let the child choose what to do. No photos, no checking, no performance. If an urgent call matters, make an exception without guilt.

The aim is not less technology for its own sake. It is fewer interruptions to a relationship we cannot replay.
