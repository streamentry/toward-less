A weak, unsweetened coffee during a workday sounds harmless—and perhaps useful. Four claims deserve separate scrutiny: better focus, less hunger, a healthier liver and lower LDL cholesterol. **The best evidence concerns filtration and short-term alertness; evidence for weight loss is much weaker.**

## Why a Hario V60 paper filter?

Coffee oils contain the diterpenes *cafestol* and *kahweol*, which can increase LDL cholesterol. Paper filters remove most of these compounds. A Hario V60 and a Chemex are simply convenient paper-filtered pour-over brewers; the V60 brand itself has no demonstrated special medical benefit.

A 2025 Swedish study measured median cafestol concentrations of **176 mg/L** in samples from 11 workplace brewing machines, versus **12 mg/L** in five homemade paper-filtered samples. These are small-sample chemical measurements, **not a clinical trial demonstrating a particular reduction in LDL** when switching to V60. Extraction and portion size matter. [1] Paper filtration primarily avoids an LDL-raising exposure; it does not actively treat high cholesterol.

## Fatty liver: a promising association, not a cure

The 2023 AASLD guidance and 2024 European EASL–EASD–EASO guidelines discuss observational associations between coffee consumption and less advanced liver disease. The association appears more consistent for **fibrosis** than for a direct reduction in liver fat. Observational data cannot establish causality, and randomized evidence remains insufficient. [2][3] Coffee should not be presented as treatment for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD). Nutrition, movement and weight management when indicated remain central.

## Appetite and fat loss: mixed evidence

A 2017 review found that caffeine taken before meals **might** transiently reduce food intake, but studies of coffee, hunger and total energy intake are inconsistent. [4] One small 2024 study observed increased desire for sweet foods in some participants; it cannot settle the question for everyone. [5] If black coffee replaces a sugary drink or mindless snacking, that is a useful behavioral change—not proof that coffee burns body fat. Do not routinely replace meals with caffeine.

## Focus without stealing sleep

Caffeine can improve alertness temporarily. **Dilution changes taste, not the total caffeine extracted** from the grounds: adding water does not undo the dose. Use less coffee or decaf for less caffeine. The FDA cites **400 mg per day** as an amount generally not associated with adverse effects in most healthy adults, **not a target**. [6] A controlled study found that **400 mg six hours before bedtime** can disturb sleep. [7]

A reasonable routine: brew **10–12 g of grounds with 250–350 mL of water** through paper, without sugar or cream, in the morning; optionally have a smaller cup before noon; switch to water or decaf in the afternoon. Caffeine levels vary with beans and preparation. After several weeks, judge the habit by sleep, snack frequency and appropriate LDL or liver follow-up—not merely a feeling of energy.

**The practical conclusion:** choose V60 for its paper filter, lighter coffee for taste, and earlier coffee for sleep. LDL, fatty liver and body composition require more than a beverage.
