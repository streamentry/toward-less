A Gemini response shared as a screenshot identifies deliberate practice as the single separator of elite performers. Its useful insight is that repeating familiar work is different from training to improve.

## What the evidence says

A [2014 meta-analysis by Macnamara and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24986855/) found that practice explained only part of performance differences, varying by field. Their [2016 sports analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635591) estimated that accumulated deliberate practice explained just 1% of variation among elite athletes. This does not mean training is unnecessary: differences within an already selected elite group are not the same as the effects of training a beginner. [Ericsson and Harwell (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824411/) also disputed the broad definition of deliberate practice used in such analyses.

A [2025 Science review by Güllich and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41411418/) found that adult world-class achievement was associated with broader early experience and more gradual early progress than exceptional youth performance. These associations do not prove that breadth causes greatness, but challenge premature specialization.

## Practice the bottleneck

Choose a specific weakness, attempt a task just beyond your reliable ability, obtain credible feedback, correct the error and try again. A founder who struggles with sales might record a customer conversation with consent, examine where the buyer lost interest, and test a better question in the next conversation. Writing another product feature may feel productive without addressing that weakness.

Boredom and difficulty can accompany learning; neither is proof of learning. Protect sleep and recovery. There is no established universal limit of three to five hours of intense concentration for every person and task.

Talent, opportunity, coaching, health and chance also matter. Practice is a lever, not a guarantee of elite status. Choose a goal worth pursuing rather than making every activity a competition.

**Measure progress by what you can do differently after feedback, not by how many hours you endured.**
