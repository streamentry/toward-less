The X diagram presents quantum immortality as a guarantee: when one outcome ends in death, “you” continue in a branch where you survive. Quantum theory does not establish that conclusion.

## What the sources say

Everett’s 1957 relative-state formulation removes wavefunction collapse. Tegmark’s 1997 paper describes quantum suicide as a thought experiment and argues that an observer in a survival outcome would experience that outcome. This is the strongest version of the claim, but it is a hypothetical inference, not evidence about human survival.

## Where the leap occurs

A surviving relative state does not show that one conscious self moves from a dead branch to another. Mallah’s 2009 preprint argues that many-worlds does not imply quantum immortality, pointing to branch measure and unresolved questions about personal identity. This remains a debate about interpretation, not an established physical result.

## How to read the image

Read as a metaphor, the diagram may express a wish for continuity. It is not a safety guarantee or a reason to take risks.
