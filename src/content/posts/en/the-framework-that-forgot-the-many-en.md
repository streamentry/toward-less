Jay Yang presents PTL—power, trust, likeness—as a checklist for personal outreach, not a complete theory of influence.

- **Power** resembles Cialdini’s authority.
- **Likeness** overlaps with liking and shared identity; Cialdini treats unity as distinct from liking.
- **Trust** is not one of Cialdini’s seven principles. Mayer et al. (1995) separate perceived trustworthiness into ability, benevolence, and integrity. Yang’s belief-and-evidence definition is a shorthand, not a full model.

The article’s own summary invokes the many. I read that as social proof. Its example of offering useful work to a peer in public, with no immediate ask, fits reciprocity better than likeness. PTL has no place for either. The piece also calls attention cheap, though its distribution argument assumes attention is scarce. The clearer distinction is that production has become easier; attention remains limited.

Use PTL as a prompt, not a complete account. Name the forces it omits.
