A social post presents acting like your future self as a shortcut. The cited study tested imagining that future.

## What the study did

- In 2011, 54 Dutch-speaking participants aged 18–43 (mostly university students; 50 women and 4 men) were randomly assigned to imagine their best possible self or ordinary daily activities.
- The first group wrote realistic goals in personal life, relationships, and work, then imagined that future for five minutes a day over two weeks. They reported larger gains in optimism than the comparison group after the first session and over two weeks. Researchers used questionnaires; the optimism difference remained after they accounted for positive mood.

## What the study did not test

It did not measure behavior change, goal completion, or whether effects lasted beyond two weeks. The sample was small and mostly young, educated women, so the result may not generalize widely.

If you try it, write a realistic picture of a good future across personal life, relationships, and work, then imagine it for five minutes a day for two weeks. That follows the study’s core method. Choosing a small next action is my suggestion, not a tested part of the study. An image can guide a choice; it cannot guarantee an outcome.
