Jeff Bezos ended his 2010 Princeton address by asking what choices would matter when we look back at 80. It can clarify our values, but “we are our choices” is an exhortation, not a complete account of a life.

## The speech’s point

Princeton’s official transcript dates the address to May 30, 2010. Bezos frames it around gifts and choices: “Cleverness is a gift, kindness is a choice.” His closing line, “Build yourself a great story,” belongs to that argument about how we use what we have.

## Use the question carefully

Imagine looking back at 80. Ask:

- Which choice would I respect?
- Who else would bear its cost?
- Which ordinary responsibilities belong in the story?

## Where the idea stops

Health, birthplace, resources, and other people’s choices shape our options. Taken literally, “we are our choices” can blame people for conditions they did not choose. A meaningful life need not be dramatic. Kindness, steady care, keeping a commitment, or declining a risk may matter more than an impressive plot.

Use the question to clarify what matters, then choose within your real duties, resources, and effects on others.
