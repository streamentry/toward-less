This review checks what Sosa’s self-hypnosis essay claims and whether the study it cites supports those claims.

Sosa frames procrastination as a lack of inner authority and recommends attraction over force. He calls this “The solution is seduction.” He also claims that concentration lets the subconscious calm and direct itself. The public essay gives no step-by-step practice beyond learning to relax and concentrate.

## The cited paper tests something else

The essay embeds a post linked to Kross et al. (2014). Their paper reports seven studies (N=585) on distanced self-talk under social stress, including first impressions and public speaking. It does not test hypnosis, procrastination, or whether the subconscious executes requests. It cannot support those claims.

## What readers can keep

As a metaphor, attraction over force can prompt a useful question: how can the next step feel easier to approach? But the essay does not establish a universal cause of procrastination or a proven self-hypnosis method. Before treating the problem as an inner authority struggle, check simpler possibilities: an unclear task, a step that is too large, friction in the environment, exhaustion, or a goal you no longer value.

Sosa’s separate paid Blueprint page advertises weekly exercises and a script archive. Those materials are outside the public essay, and this review has not assessed the subscriber content.
