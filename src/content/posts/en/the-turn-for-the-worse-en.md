This update checks David Gilmore’s 9 November launch post against what Rayline’s official pages show on 11 November 2026.

Gilmore wrote: “On Wednesday, we’re launching Rayline.” The post also presents a personal account of his healthcare work and ethical choices, and says the company raised $8.5 million. Those are his claims.

## What the official pages show

Rayline now presents four products:

- **ARC** selects models and reasoning settings. Its Responses API can return a generated answer; its Routes API gives a model selection for the customer’s own inference stack.
- **Decisions API** returns structured choices, probabilities, and scores.
- **Raydock** is an open-source CLI that connects agent tools to local and cloud models.
- **Workshop** builds apps and other software in the cloud or on a desktop.

The pages show a mix of hosted services and local, open-source tools. “Decentralized” is Gilmore’s framing; the product pages describe a hybrid system, not a wholly decentralized one. Rayline’s homepage also presents its own quality and cost figures and links a technical report. I have not independently evaluated those results.

## What remains attributed

The post’s account of the earlier healthcare AI, turning down an offer, and raising $8.5 million remains Gilmore’s account here. The product pages verify Rayline’s public product lineup, not those personal or financing details, hospital adoption, or user outcomes.

As of 11 November, the launch is no longer only a promise: Rayline has public product pages. That is evidence of a product suite, not independent proof of its performance or impact.
