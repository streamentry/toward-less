*Perfect Days* (2023), directed by Wim Wenders, follows Hirayama, a toilet cleaner in Tokyo. His days include early starts, repetitive work, trees, music, and books. Unexpected encounters reveal parts of his past. [The Cannes Film Festival lists the film’s credits and synopsis](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/perfect-days/).

## Attention and work

My reading is modest: attention changes how the day feels, not the labor itself. Hirayama does his job carefully and notices ordinary details. The film shows how that attention can make a day feel spacious.

## Contentment or avoidance?

His routine can be read two ways. It gives him steadiness; it may also help him avoid pain. The film leaves his history partly unknown, so it does not settle which reading is right. Owning little cannot tell us whether someone is content or hiding from a difficult conversation.

## Beyond the film

That distinction matters beyond the film. A quiet life can be chosen; it can also become retreat. Ambition can serve family or useful work, but it can also make the next milestone a condition for feeling alive.

## A question for tomorrow

After watching, I would ask one practical question: **Which ordinary moment tomorrow am I likely to rush past?**
