At Caltech's 2024 commencement, Jensen Huang said that intelligence was not the ability he valued most. He prized the capacity to endure setbacks, work at a craft for years, and see an opening after a failure.

That is an important corrective to our fascination with talent. Hard work sustained over time can compound. A difficult season does not automatically mean a path is wrong.

But a sentence about resilience can become dangerous when stripped of its conditions. Pain is not inherently productive. Some adversity teaches; some simply harms. A founder can spend ten years building the wrong product. A person can remain in a damaging relationship and call it loyalty. Endurance is not proof that the goal is worthwhile.

We need three distinctions:

- **Resilience:** take a setback, examine the evidence, adapt, and continue.
- **Stubbornness:** take a setback, protect the original story, and continue unchanged.
- **Wisdom:** decide whether to persist, change methods, shrink the commitment, or stop.

The difference is not how much pain someone can tolerate. It is whether reality is allowed to correct the plan.

There is a useful Buddhist parallel, though this is our interpretation rather than a quotation from the Buddha. Right effort is not the worship of effort. Effort must help reduce harmful states and cultivate wholesome ones; clinging to a losing identity is not liberation.

Before making another heroic push, ask: **What am I trying to preserve? What has actually improved? What evidence would convince me to stop?** Set an observable test and a date to review it. Protect health and family obligations while you run that test.

Persist in values that deserve a lifetime. Be willing to replace methods that do not work. Sometimes the bravest act is another attempt; sometimes it is closing a chapter without calling yourself a failure.
