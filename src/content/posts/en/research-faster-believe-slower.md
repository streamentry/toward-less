In a social post circulated on November 28, 2026, Dylan Txa (@Dylan_txa_) describes using Claude to compress weeks of market research into three hours. His method combines competitor websites, earnings calls, customer reviews and community discussions, then challenges the resulting hypotheses. The post also promotes Gojiberry AI. The time-saving claim is anecdotal, not an independently established benchmark.

## Research the market you cannot see

Competitor websites reveal positioning, not necessarily purchases. Reviews favor people motivated to write them. Earnings calls reflect management's perspective. A recurring complaint may describe an opportunity, or a problem nobody will pay to fix.

Ask AI to link each finding to its source, separate observation from inference, and identify contrary evidence. Investigate why incumbents might deliberately avoid an underserved segment. Poor margins, costly onboarding or weak demand can explain an apparent gap.

## Three hours, then seven days

Use three hours as an experimental timebox: 45 minutes collecting sources, 45 identifying patterns, 30 challenging hypotheses, 30 examining pricing and switching costs, and 30 preparing buyer interviews. The schedule is a proposal, not a measured productivity result.

For the following week, a small B2B founder might aim to contact 30 qualified prospects, hold ten conversations, find five buyers with urgent pain, obtain three trial commitments and secure one paid pilot. These are suggested milestones, not statistical benchmarks. Ask about the last time the problem occurred, current spending, who approves purchases and what would prevent switching. A paid pilot is stronger evidence than praise, but does not establish retention or scalable distribution.

The original post recommends a particular prospecting vendor; this process does not require it, and its effectiveness has not been independently assessed here.

AI can help produce a coherent thesis in minutes. It can produce a coherent defense of a bad thesis just as quickly. **Use AI to compress research, not to manufacture certainty. Let buyers test your beliefs.**
