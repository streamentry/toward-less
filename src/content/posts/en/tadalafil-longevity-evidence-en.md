Tadalafil inhibits PDE5 and enhances nitric oxide–cGMP signaling. Its approximately 17.5-hour half-life supports once-daily dosing. That explains its biological plausibility—not a proven longevity effect.

## The encouraging evidence

A 2024 systematic review of 16 observational studies involving around 1.26 million people associated PDE5-inhibitor use with **22% fewer major cardiovascular events** and **30% lower all-cause mortality**. Those are relative associations, not randomized proof. Users and nonusers may differ in underlying health and access to care; the findings cannot establish that **tadalafil 5 mg daily** prevents events.

Randomized trials of PDE5 inhibitors have improved endothelial measurements, sometimes arterial stiffness and blood pressure. But **a biomarker is not a heart attack prevented**. The drugs, doses and populations vary.

## What about the brain?

A 2026 MRI substudy of ETLAS-2 reported greater cerebral blood flow after **20 mg/day for three months** in patients with cerebral small-vessel disease. The parent randomized trial found **no clear cognitive benefit**. The participants had cerebrovascular disease, and 20 mg is not 5 mg. Neither better focus nor dementia prevention in healthy adults follows from these data. Evidence for improved muscle performance is also inadequate.

## The risks and the decision

Tadalafil can cause headache, dyspepsia, back pain and low blood pressure. **Nitrates and riociguat must not be combined with it**; recent cardiovascular events and kidney or liver impairment require medical assessment. Rare serious vision or hearing events have been reported. Daily 2.5–5 mg is an established regimen for certain conditions, **not an approved longevity protocol**.

If there is an established indication, discuss treatment and interactions with a clinician. For a healthy person seeking longer life, the benefit remains unproven. Blood-pressure management, exercise, lipid control and avoiding tobacco have stronger outcome evidence.

**Mechanism → biomarker → association is not the same as demonstrated prevention.** The right next experiment is a randomized trial measuring disease and mortality, not a lifetime prescription based on a plausible story.
