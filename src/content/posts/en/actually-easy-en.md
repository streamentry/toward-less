At Imagination in Action’s Davos summit on 21 January 2026, Eric Schmidt called it “actually easy” to make money by building an AI agent company. He added, “you can probably build one incredible company and own it yourself.” The quote is encouragement, not evidence that the work or market is easy. [Forbes’s account of the panel](https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnwerner/2026/01/29/ai-changes-global-landscape-rubenstein-hosts-schmidt-and-ucuzoglu/) includes the full context.

Schmidt’s advice was specific: build an agent to do a job, not just tools for building agents. He predicted that everyone would build agents over the next year or two. That signals competition, not guaranteed demand.

## What the numbers show

- McKinsey’s August 2026 survey of 1,719 respondents found that 37% said AI had contributed to their organization’s EBIT; 20% said operating costs constrained AI use. This covered AI broadly, not agent startups. [Survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- Gartner’s June 2025 forecast said over 40% of AI agent projects could be canceled by the end of 2027 because of costs, unclear business value, or risk controls. It is a forecast about projects, not an observed startup failure rate. [Forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)

## What “easy” leaves out

A business must do more than run. An agent has to complete a useful job reliably, fit a buyer’s workflow, and create value someone will pay for. Founders still need distribution, integrations, trust, support, and risk controls.

These facts do not prove a new agent company will fail. They do make “easy money” a poor reading of the quote.

A useful test is concrete: choose one costly task, show that an agent can complete it reliably, and ask a buyer to pay for a small pilot.
