Praise is not a commitment. A signup is not yet a benefit.

In [*Make Something People Actually Want*](https://x.com/maxtorchbearer/status/2119539599311782088), Max Torchbearer describes three ways a product can miss what people really came for.

## 1. Watch what people will give up

Friends love the idea of a weekly evening for unfinished projects. But when Wednesday at seven arrives, who actually shows up?

Don't book six weeks of sessions. Try one evening. See who comes—and, more importantly, who returns.

**Enthusiasm is a statement. Demand shows up in action and the costs someone accepts.**

## 2. Help them cross the first barrier

A meetup for people struggling to make friends fails if newcomers must already know how to approach strangers.

Greeting each person, making introductions, and starting a shared activity are not decorative features. They may be the entire value.

Ask: *What must a user already be able to do before this helps? Is that exactly what they struggle with?*

## 3. Don't automate away the thing they came to learn

A coding app that fixes every error can help beginners finish more projects. But if their goal is independent debugging, the app removes the very practice they need.

An AI assistant could point toward the mistake, give a hint, and reveal the fix only after an attempt.

**The same feature can help someone who wants speed and harm someone who wants mastery.**

Before building, test three things: what users will trade, where they get stuck, and which difficulty is actually part of the value.

Don't optimize clicks when people came for a change in their lives.
