Dan Koe’s X Article proposes a one-day exercise for examining goals and the identity behind daily habits. It has three parts:

- Morning: name the dissatisfaction you keep tolerating, picture an ordinary day if nothing changes, and describe the life you want.
- During the day: use reminders to notice whether your actions move toward or away from that direction.
- Evening: identify recurring patterns and set goals for one year, one month, and tomorrow.

The exercise can organize reflection. It is not a tested method for changing identity or guaranteeing an outcome. It assumes you can describe a life you want, but behavior is also shaped by health, money, family duties, work, and other constraints. One day of reflection cannot show whether a goal is achievable.

Two cautions matter:

- A negative vision can motivate change, but living mainly to escape a feared future can become another kind of avoidance.
- A self-chosen goal can still crowd out rest, relationships, and time without a purpose. A life does not have one universal “win” state.

Use the prompts to choose one small action you can test. Notice what changes, then revise the plan. Not every obstacle is hidden avoidance, and a major change rarely fits inside one day.
