Jash Dholani’s X Article calls this the “toys theory of greatness”: a childhood obsession can point toward adult work. Following curiosity is useful; these stories do not show that a toy causes or predicts achievement.

## What the records show

- Wilbur and Orville Wright wrote in 1908 that their father gave them a rubber-band toy helicopter in 1878. They played with it and later built larger versions. The National Park Service says their serious aviation research began after Otto Lilienthal died in 1896; they studied earlier work and wrote to the Smithsonian in 1899.
- Dholani also mentions Matisse’s puppets. I could not verify that origin story. MoMA’s timeline records that Matisse received oil paints while recovering from appendicitis in 1890, at age 20, and painted a still life that June.
- Dholani attributes Einstein’s compass story to his 1949 *Autobiographical Notes*. The accessible 1979 Archive edition is marked access-restricted, so I could not verify that passage directly.

## What stories cannot prove

These are selected stories about people who became famous, not a comparison with everyone who loved a toy. A childhood fascination may reveal an interest; it does not establish that the toy caused later achievement or predicts success.

## Keep the useful part

If an interest still matters to you, try a small project and see whether your curiosity survives the work. Treat the toy as a clue about what holds your attention, not as a formula for greatness.
