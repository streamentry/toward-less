In 2015, shortly after turning thirty, Sam Altman published [*The Days Are Long but the Decades Are Short*](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-days-are-long-but-the-decades-are-short): thirty-six lessons from the decade behind him.

Much of the advice is familiar—work hard, keep learning, take chances, care for your health. Read together, though, the points reveal a useful tension.

## Ambition needs a boundary

Altman urges us to choose worthwhile work, excel at it, and seize opportunities. He also warns against working so hard that the rest of life passes unnoticed.

Money can buy freedom; freedom from worrying about rent matters more than owning a private jet. The message is not to abandon ambition. It is to remember **what ambition is for**.

## What cannot be reclaimed

A visit postponed. An evening with someone we love exchanged for one more task of uncertain importance.

Among his thirty-six lessons, Altman puts family, friendship, and love near the top. That may be the hardest advice for people who always have something else to build.

Instead of asking only, *What will I achieve in ten years?*

Ask, *Whom will I wish I had spent more time with?*

## An ordinary day

Today may feel long, filled with small obligations. Yet a decade consists of precisely these days.

We need not turn every minute into an achievement. Nor should we use life's brevity as a reason to postpone what matters.

**Do not build a future worth living at the cost of living the days that build it.**
